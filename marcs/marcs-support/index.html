Troubleshooting

Common problems

The checks MARCS uses depend on how your institution has set up attendance. They can include a session PIN, location, Bluetooth classroom proximity, and a selfie with face and liveness verification. Settings steps are for iPhone with iOS 18 or later. On earlier versions, open Settings and scroll down to MARCS.

Signing in and account access

Students Teachers

  • Sign in with the account your institution gave you. MARCS accounts are provided by your institution.
  • Check your email address and password carefully, including capital letters and extra spaces.
  • Make sure your phone is connected to the internet.
  • If you still can’t sign in, or your account is missing or disabled, contact your institution’s authorised administrator.

Joining an attendance session

Students Teachers

  • Check the session PIN with your teacher and enter it exactly as shown.
  • Make sure the session is still active. A PIN works only while your teacher’s session is open.
  • Confirm that you are enrolled in the class the session is for. If the class is missing, ask your teacher or administrator.
  • Teachers: check that the session was started for the right class and has not ended.

Location permission

Students Teachers

Location permission may be needed to confirm that you are in the classroom when attendance is taken.

  1. Open Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services and turn Location Services on.
  2. Open Settings › Apps › MARCS › Location and choose While Using the App.
  3. On the same screen, turn on Precise Location.
  4. Return to MARCS and try again. If you tapped “Don’t Allow” earlier, iPhone won’t ask again, so use these steps.

Bluetooth and classroom proximity

Students Teachers

Depending on your institution’s setup, MARCS may use Bluetooth to check that your phone is near the classroom.

  1. Turn Bluetooth on in Settings › Bluetooth.
  2. Allow MARCS to use Bluetooth when asked, or turn it on in Settings › Apps › MARCS.
  3. On iPhone, the proximity check may also need location access. Follow the location steps too.
  4. Follow the in-app prompts, keep the attendance screen open and stay in the classroom while the check runs.

Selfie, face verification and liveness

Students

If your institution uses a selfie, face verification or liveness check, follow the on-screen instructions.

  • Allow camera access when asked, or turn on Settings › Apps › MARCS › Camera.
  • Find good, even light. Face the light instead of having a window or lamp behind you.
  • Keep your whole face visible inside the frame and hold the phone steady at eye level. Remove sunglasses or a face mask if the app can’t see your face.
  • When asked to blink or complete another liveness step, do it naturally and wait for the app to confirm.
  • If verification keeps failing, stop and contact your teacher or MARCS support.

Attendance not recorded

Students

  • Read the status message in the app. It usually explains which step did not complete.
  • Check that the session is still active. Attendance can’t be marked after it ends.
  • Check your attendance history in the app to see whether it was recorded.
  • Don’t keep retrying. Tell your teacher while you are still in class, as they may be able to record your attendance another way. If the problem continues, contact MARCS support.

Before you contact us

What to include in your request

These details help us find the problem quickly.

  • Your role: student or teacher.
  • What happened: a short description of the problem and any error message.
  • When it happened: the approximate date and time.
  • Your device: the iPhone model and iOS version, shown in Settings › General › About.
  • A screenshot of the error, if appropriate.

Keep your information safe

  • Never send your password or one-time codes (OTPs) to anyone, including support staff.
  • Don’t send selfies, face photos or other biometric data by email or phone.
  • Before sending a screenshot, crop out your face, passwords and other people’s details.
  • Share only the personal information needed to describe the problem.
